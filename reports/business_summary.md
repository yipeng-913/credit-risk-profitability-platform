# Business summary

The platform connects offline repayment-difficulty evidence with transparent hypothetical loan economics. It can help a product manager ask how ranking, calibration, funding assumptions and loss severity change a scenario. All current evidence uses synthetic data.

Three practical recommendations:
1. Validate affordability and external-score signals with authorized timestamped data before using them in a pilot. The synthetic model's importance is not evidence of real borrower drivers.
2. Review calibration and error consequences by segment; agree a documented cost framework and fairness criteria before choosing any operational threshold. The demo threshold misses many positive events and does not justify decisions.
3. Stress both risk and funding cost when discussing pricing; collect actual recovery, cash-flow and operating-cost data before interpreting the illustrative profit as a product forecast.

Top limitations: ambiguous historical as-of semantics and a non-regulatory target; synthetic-only performance evidence with no out-of-time validation; hypothetical economics without realized profit or validated loss severity. Additional concerns include policy/label shift, sensitive-feature proxies and minority-group uncertainty.

Explain to a product manager: ROC-AUC measures ranking, calibration asks whether score ranges match event frequencies, and profit here answers a hypothetical sensitivity question. A 20% score must not be described as a validated 20% chance of regulatory default within a year.

Do not auto-approve or reject real loans. Before a bank-data pilot, establish lawful data access, event/horizon definitions, as-of snapshots, out-of-time and segment validation, independent challenge, fairness/privacy review, human escalation, monitoring, rollback, actual financial labels and model governance approval. Performance deterioration could follow distribution or label shift, altered underwriting policy or changed economic conditions.
